Parish Directory

Court reporting coverage across Louisiana's 64 parishes.

Where local CCR-certified reporters are common, where travel fees usually apply, and what to expect by region.

Court reporting in Louisiana concentrates around the state's major legal markets: Orleans/Jefferson, East Baton Rouge, Caddo, Lafayette, and Calcasieu. Outside those parishes, finding a local CCR-certified reporter often means working with a statewide agency that maintains real local relationships rather than flying someone in.

This directory groups Louisiana's 64 parishes by region. For each region, we note where coverage is dense, where it's thinner, and what attorneys should plan for when scheduling.

Greater New Orleans Region

The densest court reporting market in the state. Same-day and next-day availability is common. Real-time, video, and remote depositions are routinely supported. Conference room availability in the Central Business District is generally walk-in level. Expect competitive standard rates and reasonable expedite premiums.

Capital Region

Baton Rouge supports a robust state-court and federal-court practice (Middle District of Louisiana sits here). Coverage is strong and competitive. Out-of-parish travel into the surrounding rural parishes is typically billed from Baton Rouge.

Acadiana Region

Lafayette is the legal hub of south-central Louisiana and a base for several statewide reporting agencies. Strong coverage across Lafayette and the immediately surrounding parishes; the agencies headquartered here tend to serve Lake Charles, Houma, and rural south Louisiana without travel premiums.

Southwest Louisiana

Lake Charles anchors this region, with steady volume from energy-sector litigation and personal injury matters. Local coverage in Calcasieu and Cameron is reliable; the rural parishes to the north typically draw from Lafayette or Lake Charles.

Bayou Region (South Central)

Houma and Thibodaux drive most of the volume. Energy and maritime litigation are well represented. Coverage is steady; for matters far down the bayou, plan for travel time on either reporter or attorney.

Alexandria is the regional hub. Coverage is decent for Rapides itself; surrounding parishes often draw reporters from Alexandria or Lafayette. Federal Western District of Louisiana matters often originate here.
